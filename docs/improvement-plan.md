# Duracash improvement plan: from working concept to a product a contractor can use alone

Written 2026-10-27 from seven code reviews (first run, daily use, import and data, phone and UI, code health, reliability and security, competitive gap), the 53-item punch list in `docs/client-ready-tuning.md` (ids A1-F9 below; A7 + B12 + C8 + D8 + E9 + F9) and `testdata/test-contractor/APP-ISSUES.md` (ids AI-1..AI-5). Every item was checked against the code as of this date; line numbers are from `static/app.js`, `domain.py`, `server.py` and the other modules named. Items the code did not confirm were dropped, and findings that appeared in more than one review were merged into one item that lists everything it absorbs.

The owner's goal, in his words: make it as easy as possible for a contractor to get on board and use the product. That is the ordering rule throughout: first-run and day-to-day ease first, completeness second, internal tidiness only where it makes the first two faster.

Effort: **S** under half a day, **M** one to three days, **L** a week or more. Round 1 is one to three weeks of work (four M items plus the start of the client split); each later round is a few days to a week. Every round leaves the product shippable (`python3 -m unittest discover -s tests`, 274 tests today, must stay green after every item).

## 1. Where the product stands

**Strengths.** The money loop works end to end and is more careful than most early products: a pay-app gate that refuses to send with missing paperwork, a G702/G703-pattern PDF with continuation sheet, stored materials and change-order lines, per-pay-app waiver tracking, read-only QuickBooks reading with id-safe matching, signed change orders with hashed snapshots, a hash-chained audit trail, PBKDF2 at 600k rounds, hashed sessions, a strict CSP, atomic writes, WAL SQLite, safe tar restore and encrypted off-site copies. The server has one route table with handler-existence tests and a single `GateError` type for every business refusal. The phone shell is real: drawer navigation, 44 px targets, tables that stack into cards, an in-page confirm dialog, a share target. The old name is gone from everything a customer sees; it survives only in five compatibility shims (`store.py:29,106`, `backup.py:45`, `storage.py:17-20`, `render.yaml:4`, `README.md:20`) and one default path in `scripts/video/build.py:23`.

**The biggest gaps, in plain words.**

1. **The first ten minutes punish mistakes.** The New job form has eleven fields, six required, two of them ("Approver", "AP email") words a remodeler billing a homeowner does not use, and one wrong field wipes everything typed (`app.js:1104-1108` re-renders the page; the inputs carry no `value`). The sign-up form does the same. The "Notes for the job record" textarea silently becomes the customer's required paperwork list and blocks the first send (`domain.py:1589-1591`).
2. **The first pay app is always "Blocked".** A homeowner job drafts with four required documents and no "not needed" path; a GC job is blocked on "AIA-style pay app" and "Schedule of values", both of which Duracash itself produces (`domain.py:1591`, `1461-1463`). Files already dropped on the job never satisfy an item by themselves. The pill is red.
3. **Sending is one tap with nothing to review, and may silently not go.** The job page's "Approve & send" posts immediately (`app.js:655, 894`). Today's "Review & send" opens the job page with `data-focus=".send-check"` (`app.js:601`), but that card exists only on the pay-app page (`app.js:2428-2437`), so the focus finds nothing and the next tap is the one-tap send (the comment at `app.js:572-575` still says the job page shows the card). An unverified email parks the message in the Outbox while the pay app is marked sent (`mailer.py:47`, `domain.py:434`); the sign-up reply's `verification` field is ignored by the client.
4. **Month two has dead ends.** Recording progress while a draft is open says "Saved" and leaves the draft stale; after voiding a draft the amount can only go up; a sent pay app cannot be pulled back; a short-paid pay app can never be settled; a closed job or a mistaken "Mark done" cannot be undone; pay-app numbers skip after a void (`domain.py:217-249, 304, 1258-1267`; no settle/reopen action in `JOB_ACTIONS`).
5. **Imports have two day-one traps.** The payments CSV reader ignores the Transaction Type column, so an Invoice row from the default QuickBooks report is recorded as a payment of itself, and "already recorded" is keyed on the check number across every customer (`qbo_csv.py:23-29, 106-114`). Notes lines starting with 10/16 or 1/2 are read as fractions (`importer.py:268`).
6. **The words are the onboarding, and they are mixed.** "Packet", "Mark present", "pencil copy", "playbook", "Match payment", "Close for record", "Money file", "AP email", "payer" vs "customer". Explanations live in `title=` tooltips a phone never shows (`app.js:2237`).
7. **A handful of security and recovery gaps a first paying client would not forgive.** Password reset bypasses two-step sign-in (`server.py:1217`); invited teammates arrive unverified and cannot send (`auth.py:566-577`); members can export every payer token (`server.py:2131`); signup has no rate limit; no HSTS; a restore can leave a once-deleted company silently unwritable; a missing company is replaced by a blank one and saved.

Most of this is small, well-located work. What makes it slow today is the client: one 3,146-line module, a 450-line click handler with about 100 string-compared branches, a full-page `innerHTML` re-render on every change, seven hand-rolled money parsers and validation written twice with different wording. Round 1 fixes that first so Rounds 2-6 are each a few hours.

## 2. The target experience

### The target first run (fifteen minutes, phone or laptop)

Maria runs a six-person tile company. She opens duracash.com on her phone. Under the pitch she sees "Start free" and "Look around a sample company". She taps Start free: name, email, password, a terms box. Company name is optional. The next screen says "We sent a confirmation link to maria@... You can set up your first job meanwhile", with a resend link.

Today shows one card, "Get your first pay app out", step 0 is "Add your company address, phone and licence (they print on every pay app)". Step 1 opens New job with five fields: Job name, Address, Customer, "Who pays you?" (The homeowner or property owner / A general contractor), Contract amount, which accepts "$48,500". Everything else is under "More details (optional)". She mistypes nothing, but if she had, the field would turn red with a message under it and nothing else would move.

The job page has one big button: "Bill this job". She picks "All of it: mark the job done" or types this draw. The draft appears with an amber "Needs paperwork" pill, not a red "Blocked", and the paperwork list shows two items for a homeowner: Signed contract and Completion photos. The contract PDF she dropped on the job a minute ago is already attached ("Attached from the job record"). On the photos item she taps "Take a photo". A lien waiver item is not there because her homeowner never asked for one; if it were, "Not needed for this customer" would remove it for good.

"Review & send" opens one card: To (the homeowner's email, which she is asked for right here because she skipped it), Amount, Attached (3), "Your company address is on this PDF". Approve & send is disabled with "Confirm your email first so the pay app goes out from you" until she taps the link in her inbox. Then it sends, and a banner says "Emailed to jones@..., 3 attachments".

### The target month

Every morning Today shows at most three things above the fold: "PA-OAK-02 didn't go out: email bounced. Send again", "Follow-up due on PA-MILL-01, $12,000, sent 9/30. Send follow-up / Spoke to them...", "Oak St has no pay app yet: bill the work done so far". Nothing is red unless an unsigned change order is on a bill or a payer sent one back.

Mid-month Maria realises Oak St is at 60%, not 40%. She opens Progress, sees "Billed so far $19,400 of $48,500", types the total, and the open draft is replaced at the new amount with the same number. A GC pays $9,500 on a $10,000 pay app and says the rest is a backcharge; she taps "Accept short pay", gives the reason, the pay app is paid and the waiver step opens. The GC's AP approves PA-03 on the payer page; Today stops asking her to follow up and shows "Approved by Tallow Creek, expect payment Nov 14". The bookkeeper pastes the QuickBooks payments export; Invoice rows are skipped with a note, matched payments are ticked, the two that need a pay app picked show a dropdown right in the row. At month end she downloads one pay app as a QuickBooks invoice CSV instead of re-keying it. The job that is finished gets "Finish job", with a confirm, and if a late retainage check arrives, "Reopen" is right there.
